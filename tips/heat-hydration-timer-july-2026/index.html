Use a hydration timer in extreme heat

During hot outdoor work, exercise, or chores, do not wait until thirst reminds you. Set a repeating timer and take small, regular water breaks before dehydration builds.

Before activity: Start hydrated and place cool water where it is easy to reach. Move strenuous activity to the coolest available part of the day when possible.
For moderate hot-weather activity under two hours: NIOSH recommends about 1 cup (8 ounces) of water every 15–20 minutes. A phone or watch timer can make this easier to remember.
For prolonged heavy sweating: Water alone may not replace lost electrolytes. OSHA and NIOSH advise access to balanced electrolyte drinks for work lasting two hours or more or sweating that continues for several hours.
Avoid extremes: Do not force large amounts at once. NIOSH says fluid intake generally should not exceed 6 cups per hour. Avoid alcohol, and limit very sugary or highly caffeinated drinks during heat exposure.
Add cooling and rest: Hydration is not enough by itself. Take breaks in shade or air conditioning, reduce pace, and use a buddy system so someone can notice early symptoms.
Individual medical advice matters: People with heart failure, kidney disease, fluid restrictions, or medicines that affect fluid balance should ask a clinician how much to drink during hot weather.
Stop activity and move to a cool place for weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, or fainting. Confusion, collapse, seizures, or very high body temperature may signal heat stroke—call emergency services immediately and begin rapid cooling.
