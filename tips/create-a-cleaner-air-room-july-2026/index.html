Create one cleaner-air room

When wildfire smoke raises outdoor particle pollution, concentrate your protection in one room: keep it closed, filter its air continuously, and avoid activities that create more indoor particles.

Choose the room: Pick a room large enough for your household to spend time comfortably. Close its windows and doors as much as possible while still staying safely cool.
Filter continuously: Use a portable air cleaner sized for the room, run it on the highest comfortable fan setting, and choose a model that does not intentionally produce ozone.
Use the HVAC system wisely: If compatible with your system, use a MERV 13 or higher-efficiency filter and set the fan to run more often. Check the filter frequently during heavy smoke and replace it when dirty.
Do not add particles indoors: Avoid smoking or vaping, candles, incense, fireplaces, aerosol sprays, and high-temperature cooking such as frying while smoke is present.
Have a temporary backup: If a commercial cleaner is unavailable or unaffordable, EPA says a properly built box-fan-and-filter cleaner can be a temporary option. Use a certified 2012-or-newer fan and follow official fire-safety instructions.
Heat still matters: Do not remain in a sealed room that is becoming dangerously hot. If you cannot stay both cool and away from smoke, seek a cleaner-air shelter, library, cooling center, or another air-conditioned location.
One well-filtered room can be more practical than trying to clean the entire home during a smoke event.
