Never test a flooded road

When water covers a road, sidewalk, underpass, or low crossing, do not try to judge whether it is shallow enough. Turn around immediately, choose another route, and move toward higher ground.

Do not drive through it: Water can hide a washed-out road, debris, open drains, or downed power lines. A vehicle may stall, float, or be swept away before the driver can react.
Do not walk through it: The National Weather Service says only 6 inches of fast-moving water can knock over an adult. Ready.gov says 1 foot of moving water can sweep away a vehicle.
Respect every barrier: Never drive around a road-closed sign or barricade. Local responders may know about damage or current that is not visible from your position.
Use a safer route: Delay the trip, reroute, or stay where you are if local officials advise it. During a warning, avoid low spots, drainage channels, underpasses, creeks, and bridges over fast-moving water.
If water rises around you: Follow emergency alerts and move to higher ground. If a vehicle is trapped in rapidly moving water, Ready.gov advises staying inside; if water is rising inside, get onto the roof and signal or call for help.
After contact with floodwater: CDC warns that it can contain sewage, chemicals, sharp debris, animals, and electrical hazards. Keep children away, clean exposed skin, and seek medical care for contaminated wounds or illness.
A delayed or canceled trip is safer than testing floodwater. Turn Around, Don’t Drown®.
