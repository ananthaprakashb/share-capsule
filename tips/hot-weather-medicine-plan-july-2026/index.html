Make a hot-weather medicine plan

Before the next very hot day, spend five minutes checking whether any medicine or medical device you use needs special handling in heat. Ask a pharmacist or clinician what to do—do not stop or change treatment on your own.

Make one current list: Write down prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, dose, purpose, and any refrigerated medicine or powered medical device. Keep the list in your phone and emergency kit.
Ask one focused question: “Could heat, dehydration, or heavy sweating change how this medicine affects me?” CDC notes that some commonly used medicines can increase dehydration, reduce sweating, or otherwise raise heat-illness risk.
Read the storage label: Keep medicines in the temperature range stated on the package. Do not leave them in a parked car, direct sun, near a hot appliance, or in another place exposed to extreme temperatures.
Plan for a power outage: Know which medicines or devices require refrigeration or electricity. Keep the refrigerator closed during an outage and ask the pharmacist or manufacturer in advance how long the specific product can safely remain outside its normal range.
After heat exposure, verify: If a medicine has been exposed to excessive heat, do not guess that it is fine. Contact a pharmacist, clinician, or manufacturer for product-specific advice and replacement guidance.
Important: Drinking extra water is not safe for everyone. People with heart failure, kidney disease, fluid restrictions, or other medical conditions should follow their clinician’s individualized hydration advice.
Never stop or change a medicine because of hot weather without professional advice. Seek urgent help for confusion, fainting, seizures, very high body temperature, or other possible signs of heat stroke.
